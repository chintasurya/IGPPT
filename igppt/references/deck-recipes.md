# Deck recipes

Proven storylines for common requests. Adapt the slide count to the request; each line is one slide as `type: message`.

## Status update / QBR (6 to 10 slides)
1. title
2. stats: the headline results (3 or 4 numbers against target)
3. cards: what we delivered (3 items)
4. chart: the trend that matters, with takeaways
5. table or stats: service levels or KPIs against target
6. timeline: next quarter's plan
7. bullets with callout: risks and the decisions needed
8. closing

## Proposal / solution design (10 to 16 slides)
Lessons from IG proposals: lead with the client's problem, back every claim with their data, show the team can actually carry the load, and keep commercials consistent.
1. title
2. agenda
3. bullets or comparison: our understanding (today vs target state, the pains behind the request)
4. stats: what their data shows (volumes, mix, peaks), with the source in `note`
5. cards: the solution in 3 to 5 parts
6. process: transition plan (onboard, knowledge transfer, co-support, take ownership, optimise)
7. process: how work moves between teams (handoff steps, who owns each, when ownership transfers)
8. cards: scope rule (what the team handles vs what goes back), with examples
9. team: roles; or a custom `schedule` slide via ig_kit for shift coverage, backups and on-call
10. table: service levels and KPIs (mark targets "proposed" until agreed)
11. cards or stats: value to the client (each tied to a number from their data)
12. bullets: risks and mitigations
13. table: commercials (check the arithmetic: monthly x 12 = annual)
14. timeline: next steps to signature and go-live
15. closing

Before sizing a team, compare demand with capacity: tickets x handling time + enhancement hours + overheads, against about 135 productive hours per person per month. Say plainly if the team is short and show options with cost.

## Project kickoff (6 to 9 slides)
title, statement (why this project), cards (objectives), process (approach/phases), timeline (milestones), team (who does what), table (ways of working: meetings, tools, reporting), bullets (risks and dependencies), closing

## Project spotlight (1 slide, or 10 to 12 for a deep dive)
For a PM presenting a project, or one slide per project in a portfolio deck. Start from `examples/project-spotlight-template.json` (blank, with field guidance in the speaker notes) or `examples/project-spotlight-sample.json` (filled, fictional).
1. title
2. snapshot: the one-page slide. Tiles: practice, team size, project length, ACV, TCV. Cards: customer problem, scope, technologies (chips), how we found the need, Deal of Delivery (DOD), outcomes (optional)
3. section: deep dive
4. stats: engagement at a glance (team size, length, ACV, TCV; check ACV x years = TCV)
5. comparison: the customer problem, before vs after
6. comparison: in scope vs out of scope
7. cards: technologies by layer (cloud, application, data and AI, DevOps)
8. process: how we identified the need (trigger, discovery, business case, approval)
9. bullets with callout: Deal of Delivery (created yes / no, commitments, who agreed, where stored)
10. stats: outcomes so far (optional; measured results only)
11. closing
For a portfolio deck: title, agenda, then one `snapshot` per project, closing.

## Product or tool demo (6 to 9 slides)
title, agenda, cards (what it is: 4 capabilities), stats (where it helps, as percentages), cards (the scenarios you will show: ticket, fix, result), section "LIVE DEMO" (leave on screen while switching), stats or bullets (recap), timeline or process (next steps), closing. Put every explanatory slide before the live part so the presenter switches screens once.

## Sales pitch / capability overview (5 to 8 slides)
title, statement (the client's problem in one line), cards (how we solve it), stats (proof: results for similar clients, anonymised), process (how we engage), team, closing

## Training session (6 to 12 slides)
title, agenda, cards (what you will learn), process (step-by-step task), image (screenshots of each step), table (quick reference), bullets (common mistakes), closing

## All-hands / team update (5 to 8 slides)
title, stats (wins), cards (highlights), team (welcomes or recognition), timeline (what is next), statement (the one thing to remember), closing

## One-pager / executive summary (1 to 3 slides)
cards or stats with a `note` carrying the ask; no title or closing slide unless requested.

## Defaults when the request is vague
- Audience unknown: write for a busy senior reader; numbers first.
- Length unknown: 8 slides.
- No data supplied: use clearly marked placeholders `[XX]` and list them in your reply.
- No date: use the current month and year.
